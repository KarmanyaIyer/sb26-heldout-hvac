The Smart Buildings Challenge scores a single HVAC control policy on buildings and climates it has never seen. Its development leaderboard uses six hidden buildings in five climates and a simulator with known artifacts that the final round will remove. We build a held-out evaluation closer to that final round: 16 scenarios from 8 newly generated buildings, rescaled into the public size range and paired with unused weather months, run on a simulator with two community-proposed physics fixes. On it we analyze the strongest public controller we found, a one-step model-predictive controller with online per-zone system identification and supervisory hot-water logic. Its cost weights barely matter: correcting the gas-to-electricity price from 0.30 to the reward's 0.48 to 0.55, or doubling the comfort price, changes no scenario's 30-day score by more than 0.05\%. Three small changes to start-up timing, hot-water standby and the unoccupied cooling ceiling improve the held-out score by 0.27\% (95\% building-bootstrap interval 0.07 to 0.52\%) on the corrected simulator but worsen it by 7.1\% on the shipped one. That loss comes entirely from shortening the public controller's 72-hour hot-water hold, consistent with the hold drawing much of its value from a tank billing artifact. On the 30 public scenarios the changes do not carry over (0.44\% worse, interval 0.19\% better to 0.80\% worse): the lower cooling ceiling costs up to 4.9\% in the highest-load Phoenix episodes, which val16 under-samples, and without it the other two changes improve the score by 0.19\%. Analysis-only oracles that read the simulator's per-zone occupancy improve on the public controller by 5 to 7\%, while our test detects no trace of the 70 W occupant heat gain in the controller's model residual. A simple tank-sawtooth rule gains 7.2\% on the shipped simulator and 0.3\% on the corrected one. On the public leaderboard, which uses the shipped simulator, our controller scores 0.01048 against 0.00988 for the public chain.
